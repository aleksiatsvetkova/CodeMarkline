## 1. Purpose, Adoption, and Definitions

### 1.1. General Applicability

This Aleksia Restricted Use Policy (the “Policy”) establishes conditions and reservations concerning access to, ordinary use of, inspection of, analysis of, and attempted alteration or reproduction of any work that includes or expressly adopts it (a “Project”). It is designed for use unchanged across Projects, regardless of their name, purpose, location, release status, format, platform, or technical implementation.

### 1.2. Owner

“aleksiatsvetkova” (the “Owner”) identifies the proprietor of original material created by, assigned to, or otherwise lawfully owned by aleksiatsvetkova. Naming the Owner does not transfer third-party property or create exclusive rights that the Owner does not possess.

### 1.3. Protected Material

“Protected Material” includes original source and executable code, protectable designs and expression, proprietary assets, documents, internal resources, qualifying confidential information, and other elements of a Project in which the Owner actually holds applicable intellectual property or other enforceable rights. Protection is not dependent on whether material is public, private, human-readable, compiled, bundled, or obfuscated.

### 1.4. End User and Ordinary Use

An “End User” is a person lawfully accessing or operating a Project through functionality intentionally made available by the Owner. “Ordinary Use” refers to such intended operations and their technically necessary incidental acts. End-user status creates no authority to alter Owner-controlled files or Protected Material.

### 1.5. Written Authorization

“Written Authorization” means express, verifiable, activity-specific permission issued by the Owner. Under this Policy, such permission may concern only non-modifying activities. No person or system other than the Owner receives modification authority from this Policy, whether by written access permission, employment, collaboration, technical access, or delegation.

### 1.6. Responsible Parties and Automated Systems

This Policy addresses individuals, organizations, developers, researchers, employees, contractors, service providers, operators of automated tools, and those directing or knowingly facilitating their actions. References to bots, AI systems, language models, and autonomous agents concern the legally responsible persons or entities operating or directing them; software is not treated as an independently contracting legal person.

### 1.7. Relationship to Other Terms

This Policy complements, but does not replace, applicable proprietary licenses, copyright notices, valid service agreements, security policies, or third-party license terms. Where an enforceable agreement independently governs an activity, that agreement operates within its lawful scope. This Policy itself grants no third-party right to modify the Owner's Protected Material.

## 5. Reverse Engineering, Decompilation, and Reconstruction

### 5.1. Reservation of Analysis Rights

The Owner does not grant permission to discover, derive, recover, decompile, disassemble, reconstruct, or otherwise analyze proprietary implementation details outside Ordinary Use or specifically permitted non-modifying activities, to the extent such restrictions are enforceable.

### 5.2. Restricted Activities

Without an independent lawful basis, restricted activities include:

1. Reverse-engineering Protected Material or recovering its source implementation from compiled or packaged forms.
2. Decompiling, disassembling, decoding, unpacking, or translating executable instructions into reusable implementation artifacts.
3. Deriving protected class, function, method, symbol, file, dependency, control-flow, or internal architectural structures.
4. Analyzing minified, optimized, obfuscated, packed, or transformed implementations to reconstruct original protected expression.
5. Recovering embedded algorithms as expressed in proprietary code, private data structures, protected configurations, or original resource arrangements.
6. Reconstructing functional implementations by copying protected expression or unlawfully extracted internal materials.
7. Comparing successive builds, resources, or binaries for unauthorized extraction or reconstruction of protected internal changes.
8. Producing reconstructed source projects, derivative implementation assets, or modified builds from extracted Protected Material.
9. Delegating any of the foregoing to human analysts, automated pipelines, coding assistants, or autonomous agents.

### 5.3. Scope and Legal Limits

These restrictions are not a claim of exclusive rights over unprotectable methods, observable facts, public interfaces, or independently created software. Protected observation, study, testing, interoperability, and other nonwaivable rights remain unaffected.
